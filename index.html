Overview of Species Interactions

In ecology, interspecific interactions describe how individual species within a community influence each other's fitness, survival, and reproduction. Select any Real World Example in the table below to open a detailed modal showing photos of both species, the interaction scenario, and the evolutionary mechanism driving trait adaptation over time.

Summary of Primary Interspecific Interaction Types
Interaction Type Species A Outcome Species B Outcome Definition Real World Example
Mutualism + (Benefit) + (Benefit) An interaction where both participating species experience enhanced survival and reproduction.
Commensalism + (Benefit) 0 (Neutral) One species gains a benefit (e.g., transport, shelter) while the second species is unaffected.
Parasitism + (Benefit) - (Harm) The parasite derives nutrients/shelter from the host, reducing host fitness without immediate mortality.
Herbivory + (Benefit) - (Harm) An animal consumes plant tissue, selecting for plant defense mechanisms (spines, toxins).
Interspecific Competition - (Harm) - (Harm) Two species compete for the same limiting resource (food, light, territory), penalizing both.
Mycorrhizal Mutualism + (Benefit) + (Benefit) Fungal hyphae supply soil minerals to plant roots in exchange for photosynthetic carbohydrates.
